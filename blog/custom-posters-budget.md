# Custom Posters That Actually Work: Design Tips When You're Not a Designer

You want your space to feel intentional, not generic. Custom posters nail that better than anything else—but hiring a designer costs $500+. Here's how I build posters that look professional without the price tag.

## 1. Start with Constraint, Not Blank Canvas

A blank design tool paralyzes most people. Instead, pick one constraint: 
- One dominant color
- Two complementary fonts
- A single visual element (type-only, geometric, or minimal illustration)

Constraints force decisions. Blank canvas makes you second-guess everything.

## 2. Typography Is 90% of It

If you can't draw, that's fine—most professional posters are just text. Pick:
- **One headline font** (bold, interesting, distinctive)
- **One body font** (clean, readable—Helvetica works)

Good type hierarchy beats mediocre imagery every time.

## 3. Know Your Room

A boho living room ≠ a minimalist office ≠ a creative studio. Before you design, know:
- What colors are already in the space?
- Is it warm or cool?
- Are people expected to read it or just feel it?

Design for the room, not just your taste.

## 4. One Strong Visual or None

Don't scatter icons and illustrations everywhere. Either:
- **One focal visual element** (framed typography, a bold shape, geometric pattern)
- **Pure text** with killer spacing

Mixed approach usually looks crowded.

## 5. Test It Small First

Print an 8.5×11" version at home before ordering a 24×36". You'll catch:
- Text too small to read from across the room
- Colors that look different in natural light
- Spacing that felt right digitally but looks off printed

$1 test beats $30 regret.

## 6. Your Best Resource: Printables & Templates

If you're genuinely stuck, look at what's selling on Etsy or Gumroad in your style. Not to copy—to understand what actually appeals to people. Successful design often looks simpler than you'd expect.

## The Real Secret

Most people overthink custom posters. The ones that work have:
1. Clear intent (what's the room about?)
2. Readable text (if text is present)
3. One strong decision per poster (not five competing ideas)

You don't need to be a designer. You need to be decisive.
