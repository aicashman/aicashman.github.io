The Solopreneur's Minimal Tech Stack: Run a Business Without Drowning in Tools

Published October 2, 2026 | 5 min read

Most people starting a one-person business make the same mistake: they sign up for a dozen tools, pay for all of them, and use maybe three.

Here's a leaner way to think about it.

The 4 Things You Actually Need

  1. A way to build pages/funnels — where people land and buy.
  2. Email — the only audience channel you truly own.
  3. A way to take payment — checkout that works.
  4. Something to host your product/course — if you sell one.

That's it. Everything else is optional until you have revenue.

Why "All-in-One" Beats Duct-Taping Tools

When you're solo, every integration you maintain is time you're not spending on the actual business. Stitching together:

  • a page builder +
  • separate email tool +
  • separate checkout +
  • separate course host

...means four logins, four bills, and four things that can break.

Consolidating these into one platform removes that overhead. You trade a little flexibility for a lot of saved time — a good deal when you're the whole team.

A Simple Decision Framework

  • Just validating an idea? Use the free tier of one all-in-one tool. Don't pay yet.
  • Getting first sales? Upgrade only when a limit actually blocks you.
  • Scaling? That's when specialized tools start to earn their keep.

The Honest Tradeoff

All-in-one tools are fantastic early and can feel limiting later. That's fine — by the time they limit you, you'll have revenue to afford better. Start lean, upgrade on evidence, never on FOMO.
